\begin{abstract}
We describe a formalisation, in Lean~4 over \textsf{mathlib}, of the
convergence core of the cluster expansion method for abstract polymer
systems, with no unproved placeholders. The results proved in full are: the
deletion recursion
$Z(\Lambda) = Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\}) + w(\gamma)\,
Z(\Lambda \setminus N^*(\gamma))$ for weights in an arbitrary commutative
ring; the Dobrushin convergence criterion in product form, with the ratio
bound $\lvert Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\})\rvert \le (1 + \mu(\gamma))
\lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert$; the classical Kotecký--Preiss criterion in sum
form, including its ratio bound $e^{a(\gamma)}$, obtained from the product
form by the comparison $\mu = \lvert w\rvert e^{a}$ and $1 + x \le e^{x}$;
two-sided volume-linear bounds
$\lvert \log \lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert \rvert \le \sum_{\gamma \in \Lambda}
\log(1 + \mu(\gamma))$; the hierarchy of hypotheses
Kotecký--Preiss $\Rightarrow$ Dobrushin $\Rightarrow$ Fernández--Procacci;
and the Fernández--Procacci criterion itself — nonvanishing and the ratio
bound under the weakest of the three hypotheses — following the inductive
proof of Fialho. Beyond the criteria, we formalise the first
combinatorial layer of the cluster series: Ursell functions as
alternating sums over connected spanning subgraphs of the
incompatibility graph, and Penrose's tree--graph bound
$\lvert \varphi^{T} \rvert \le \#\{\text{spanning trees}\}$, proved via a
complete formalisation of the Penrose partition scheme; and the Mayer
graph expansion of $Z$ together with the finite cluster recursion
$Z(\Lambda) = Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma_0\})
+ \sum_{\gamma_0 \in B \subseteq \Lambda} w^{B} \varphi(B)
Z(\Lambda \setminus B)$ — the exponential structure of the expansion —
over arbitrary commutative rings, together with its closed iterate, the
cluster factorisation of $Z$ over collections of pairwise disjoint
clusters — the finite exponential formula in set form. On the analytic
side, the cluster series is defined as a genuine infinite sum over
orders, proved absolutely convergent in the small-weight regime
$e \sum_{\Lambda} \lvert w \rvert < 1$, with geometric term bounds via a
root-tree count extracted from the Penrose scheme, and with an anchored
bound proportional to the weight of the anchoring polymer, uniformly in
the volume; and it is then identified with $\log Z$ itself — the
exponential formula $Z = \exp(\text{cluster series})$ in that regime,
whose combinatorial core, the multinomial count of set partitions by
block sizes, is proved from scratch.
An earlier version of this note claimed that the sum form was not
obtainable from the product form; the formalisation of the comparison
argument corrected this, and we document both the error and its repair.
The development is a step of a machine-checked treatment of the convergence
machinery underlying rigorous renormalisation group analyses of lattice
field theories.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cluster expansions are among the basic tools of rigorous statistical
mechanics and constructive field theory. In the abstract polymer formulation
one is given a finite set of \emph{polymers}, a symmetric and reflexive
\emph{incompatibility} relation, and complex or real \emph{weights}; the
object of interest is the polymer partition function, a sum over collections
of pairwise compatible polymers. The Kotecký--Preiss criterion
\cite{KP86} gives a sufficient condition for the logarithm of this partition
function to be controlled by an absolutely convergent series, uniformly in
the volume; see \cite{FV17} for a textbook treatment and \cite{FP07} for a
sharper criterion.

Two circumstances motivate a formalisation. First, applications of the
method, notably in rigorous renormalisation group analyses of lattice gauge
theories in the tradition of Balaban (see \cite{Dim13a,Dim13b,Dim13c} for a
modern exposition), rest on long chains of estimates whose unnamed constants
have, as far as we are aware, never been verified line by line by a machine;
the Liquid Tensor Experiment \cite{LTE} has demonstrated that formalisation
of proofs at this level of technical difficulty is feasible and useful.
Second, the library situation is favourable: \textsf{mathlib}
\cite{mathlib20} contains the finite set combinatorics, ordered field and
special function material needed for the statement of the criterion, and
recent formalisations in stochastic analysis \cite{BM25} and high energy
physics tooling \cite{HepLean} indicate growing infrastructure on the
mathematical physics side. To our knowledge no cluster expansion material is
currently available in \textsf{mathlib}.

The present note documents the current state. First, a machine-checked
proof of the fundamental recursion for the polymer partition function, in a
generality (commutative ring valued weights) that covers the real, complex
and formal power series cases occurring in applications. Second, a complete
proof of the Dobrushin product-form criterion: nonvanishing of $Z$ together
with the ratio bound that controls the effect of deleting a polymer. Third,
consequences extracted from the product form without further induction:
telescoped deletion bounds, two-sided volume-linear bounds on
$\log \lvert Z \rvert$, the classical Kotecký--Preiss sum-form criterion
via a comparison of hypotheses, and the hierarchy of conditions ending in
the Fernández--Procacci form. Fourth, the Fernández--Procacci criterion
itself, via the recent inductive proof of Fialho \cite{Fia20}. Fifth, the
combinatorial base of the cluster series: Ursell functions and Penrose's
tree--graph bound \cite{Pen67}, via the Penrose partition scheme
(Section~\ref{sec:ursell}). Sixth, the Mayer graph expansion \cite{MM41}
and the finite cluster recursion — the exponential structure of the
expansion — over arbitrary commutative rings
(Section~\ref{sec:mayer}). Seventh, the cluster series of $\log Z$ as an
analytic object, with absolute convergence and anchored bounds in the
small-weight regime (Section~\ref{sec:series}). Eighth, the exponential
formula (Theorem~\ref{thm:exp}), which identifies that series with
$\log Z$ in the same regime and so upgrades the volume-linear bounds of
Theorem~\ref{thm:log} to an exact expansion. Ninth, the sharp
Kotecký--Preiss summability estimate (Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp}), which
replaces the smallness hypothesis by the Kotecký--Preiss condition
itself (Section~\ref{sec:sharp}). The development is about 7500 lines of Lean in
eleven files, compiles against current \textsf{mathlib}, and contains no
\texttt{sorry} placeholders.
Section~\ref{sec:kp} documents a claim made in an earlier version of this
note that the formalisation subsequently refuted.

\section{The abstract setting}

\begin{definition}
A \emph{polymer system} on a type $\iota$ consists of a relation
$\iota \times \iota \to \{\mathrm{true},\mathrm{false}\}$, written
$\gamma \nsim \delta$, that is symmetric and satisfies
$\gamma \nsim \gamma$ for every $\gamma$. A finite set
$S \subseteq \iota$ is \emph{independent} if $\gamma \sim \delta$ for all
distinct $\gamma, \delta \in S$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $w \colon \iota \to R$, and let
$\Lambda \subseteq \iota$ be finite. The \emph{partition function} is
\[
  Z(\Lambda) \;=\; \sum_{\substack{S \subseteq \Lambda \\ S \text{
  independent}}} \; \prod_{\gamma \in S} w(\gamma),
\]
the empty set contributing the term $1$.
\end{definition}

For $\gamma \in \Lambda$ write
$N^*(\gamma) = \{\delta \in \Lambda : \delta \nsim \gamma\}$ for the closed
incompatibility neighbourhood, so that $\gamma \in N^*(\gamma)$, and
$\Lambda_\gamma = \Lambda \setminus N^*(\gamma)$ for the set of polymers in
$\Lambda$ compatible with $\gamma$.

\begin{theorem}[fundamental recursion]\label{thm:rec}
For every finite $\Lambda$ and every $\gamma \in \Lambda$,
\[
  Z(\Lambda) \;=\; Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\})
  \;+\; w(\gamma)\, Z(\Lambda_\gamma).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Split the sum defining $Z(\Lambda)$ according to whether $\gamma \in S$.
The independent sets not containing $\gamma$ are exactly the independent
subsets of $\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\}$. The independent sets containing
$\gamma$ are of the form $S = \{\gamma\} \cup T$ with
$T \subseteq \Lambda_\gamma$ independent, and this correspondence
$S \mapsto S \setminus \{\gamma\}$ is a bijection; the associated products
factor as $w(\gamma) \prod_{\delta \in T} w(\delta)$.
\end{proof}

The recursion is the engine of the ratio-telescoping induction, which
yields the following convergence criterion, stated here in the product
(Dobrushin) form; see \cite{SS05} for the surrounding theory.

\begin{theorem}[Dobrushin criterion; formalised]\label{thm:dob}
Let $w, \mu \colon \iota \to \mathbb{R}$ with $\mu \ge 0$ on $\Lambda$, and
suppose that for every $\gamma \in \Lambda$
\[
  \lvert w(\gamma)\rvert \prod_{\substack{\delta \in \Lambda \\ \delta
  \nsim \gamma}} \bigl(1 + \mu(\delta)\bigr) \;\le\; \mu(\gamma),
\]
the product running over the closed incompatibility neighbourhood. Then
$Z(\Lambda) \neq 0$, and for every $\gamma \in \Lambda$,
\[
  \lvert Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\})\rvert \;\le\;
  \bigl(1 + \mu(\gamma)\bigr)\, \lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (as formalised)]
Strong induction on $\lvert\Lambda\rvert$; the two statements are proved
simultaneously and no division occurs anywhere. Given $\gamma \in \Lambda$,
one first shows by an inner induction that deleting any set $E$ from a
proper subset $S$ costs at most the factor $\prod_{\delta \in E}(1 +
\mu(\delta))$, each single deletion being controlled by the outer induction
hypothesis. Applying this with $S = \Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\}$ and $E$
the incompatibility neighbourhood of $\gamma$ bounds
$\lvert Z(\Lambda_\gamma)\rvert$; the recursion of Theorem~\ref{thm:rec}
and the triangle inequality then give
$\lvert Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\})\rvert \le \lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert +
c\,\lvert Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\})\rvert$ with
$c = \lvert w(\gamma)\rvert \prod_{\delta \in D}(1 + \mu(\delta))$, and the
hypothesis, rewritten as $c\,(1 + \mu(\gamma)) \le \mu(\gamma)$ after
splitting off the factor for $\delta = \gamma$, closes the estimate.
Nonvanishing follows by applying the ratio bound at any single polymer.
\end{proof}

\section{The formalisation}

The development lives in eleven files inside a Lean~4 project depending
on \textsf{mathlib}: the criteria core in \texttt{ClusterExpansion.lean},
the Penrose scheme in \texttt{Ursell.lean}, the Mayer layer in
\texttt{Mayer.lean}, the analytic layer in \texttt{ClusterSeries.lean},
the exponential formula in \texttt{Exponential.lean}, and the sharp
Kotecký--Preiss estimate across \texttt{Trees.lean},
\texttt{TreeDecomp.lean}, \texttt{TreePeel.lean},
\texttt{TreeRelabel.lean}, \texttt{TreeLink.lean} and
\texttt{SharpKP.lean}. We record the design decisions that shaped
the core.

\subsection{Decidability by construction}
The incompatibility relation is carried as a \texttt{Bool}-valued function
rather than a \texttt{Prop}-valued one:

\begin{lstlisting}
structure PolymerSystem (i : Type*) where
  incomp : i -> i -> Bool
  symm   : forall g d, incomp g d = incomp d g
  refl   : forall g, incomp g g = true
\end{lstlisting}

Independence is then a decidable predicate for free, which is what permits
the partition function to be written as a sum over a filtered powerset,
\begin{lstlisting}
def Z (L : Finset i) : R :=
  Sum_{S in L.powerset.filter (fun S => Indep P S)}
    Prod_{g in S} w g
\end{lstlisting}
(displayed here in mathematical notation; the source uses the
\textsf{mathlib} big operator syntax). No classical choice is needed to
\emph{state} anything; \texttt{classical} is invoked only inside the main
proof for convenience.

\subsection{Generality of the weight ring}
Theorem~\ref{thm:rec} is purely combinatorial, and the formal statement
quantifies over an arbitrary \texttt{CommRing}. This matters for the
intended applications: cluster expansions are routinely applied with complex
weights and with weights in rings of formal power series, and the analytic
hypotheses enter only later, through the convergence criteria.

\subsection{The bijection}
The formal proof follows the paper proof. The sum is split with
\texttt{sum\_filter\_add\_sum\_filter\_not}; the first half is identified
with $Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\})$ by an extensionality argument on the
filtered powersets, using
$S \subseteq \Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\} \leftrightarrow
S \subseteq \Lambda \wedge \gamma \notin S$. The second half is treated with
\texttt{Finset.sum\_bij'}, the bijection being
$S \mapsto S \setminus \{\gamma\}$ with inverse
$T \mapsto \{\gamma\} \cup T$. The single nontrivial lemma feeding the
bijection is:

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem indep_insert_iff (hT : T <= compat P L g) :
    Indep P (insert g T) <-> Indep P T
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent
whose proof is a case analysis using symmetry of the relation. The product
identity $\prod_{S} w = w(\gamma)\prod_{S \setminus \{\gamma\}} w$ is
\texttt{Finset.prod\_insert} applied after rewriting $S$ as
\texttt{insert} $\gamma$ \texttt{(S.erase} $\gamma$\texttt{)}.

\section{Consequences of the product form}\label{sec:consequences}

The following statements are all formalised, with no further induction on
the partition function beyond Theorem~\ref{thm:dob}.

\begin{theorem}[volume-linear control of $\log \lvert Z\rvert$;
formalised]\label{thm:log}
Under the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:dob}, for every $E \subseteq
\Lambda$,
\[
  \lvert Z(\Lambda \setminus E)\rvert \;\le\;
  \Bigl(\prod_{\delta \in E} (1 + \mu(\delta))\Bigr)
  \lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert,
\]
and consequently
\[
  \Bigl(\prod_{\gamma \in \Lambda} (1 + \mu(\gamma))\Bigr)^{-1}
  \;\le\; \lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert \;\le\;
  \prod_{\gamma \in \Lambda} (1 + \mu(\gamma)),
  \qquad
  \bigl\lvert \log \lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert \bigr\rvert \;\le\;
  \sum_{\gamma \in \Lambda} \log (1 + \mu(\gamma))
  \;\le\; \sum_{\gamma \in \Lambda} \mu(\gamma).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (as formalised)]
The deletion bound telescopes the single-polymer ratio bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dob} over $E$, monotonicity of the hypothesis under
subsets supplying the induction. Taking $E = \Lambda$ and $Z(\emptyset)=1$
gives the lower bound. The upper bound is a separate induction on
$\lvert \Lambda \rvert$: the recursion, the triangle inequality and the
deletion bound give $\lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert \le (1 + \mu(\gamma))\,
\lvert Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\})\rvert$. The logarithmic bounds
follow with $\log(1+x) \le x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Kotecký--Preiss criterion; formalised]\label{thm:kp}
Let $a \colon \iota \to \mathbb{R}$ with $a \ge 0$ on $\Lambda$ and suppose
that for every $\gamma \in \Lambda$
\[
  \sum_{\substack{\delta \in \Lambda \\ \delta \nsim \gamma}}
  \lvert w(\delta)\rvert \, e^{a(\delta)} \;\le\; a(\gamma).
\]
Then $Z(\Lambda) \neq 0$, and for every $\gamma \in \Lambda$,
\[
  \lvert Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma\})\rvert \;\le\;
  e^{a(\gamma)}\, \lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert,
  \qquad
  \bigl\lvert \log \lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert \bigr\rvert \;\le\;
  \sum_{\gamma \in \Lambda} \lvert w(\gamma)\rvert\, e^{a(\gamma)} .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (as formalised)]
Set $\mu(\gamma) = \lvert w(\gamma)\rvert e^{a(\gamma)}$. Then, by
$1 + x \le e^{x}$ applied factorwise and the sum-form hypothesis,
\[
  \prod_{\delta \nsim \gamma} \bigl(1 + \mu(\delta)\bigr)
  \;\le\; \exp \Bigl( \sum_{\delta \nsim \gamma} \mu(\delta) \Bigr)
  \;\le\; e^{a(\gamma)},
\]
so $\lvert w(\gamma)\rvert \prod_{\delta \nsim \gamma}(1 + \mu(\delta)) \le
\mu(\gamma)$: the Dobrushin hypothesis holds, and Theorem~\ref{thm:dob}
applies. For the classical ratio constant, the sum at $\gamma$ contains the
term $\delta = \gamma$ (the relation is reflexive), whence
$\mu(\gamma) \le a(\gamma)$ and
$1 + \mu(\gamma) \le 1 + a(\gamma) \le e^{a(\gamma)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Fernández--Procacci condition]
With $\mu \ge 0$ on $\Lambda$: for every $\gamma \in \Lambda$,
$\lvert w(\gamma) \rvert \, \Xi_{N^*(\gamma)}(\mu) \le \mu(\gamma)$, where
$\Xi_A(\mu) = \sum_{I \subseteq A \text{ independent}} \prod_{\delta \in I}
\mu(\delta)$ is the independence polynomial of $A$ — in the development
literally $Z$ evaluated at the weights $\mu$, so no new definition is
needed.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[hierarchy of hypotheses; formalised]\label{thm:hier}
Kotecký--Preiss $\Rightarrow$ Dobrushin (with
$\mu = \lvert w \rvert e^{a}$) $\Rightarrow$ Fernández--Procacci. The
second implication rests on $\Xi_A(\mu) \le \prod_{\delta \in A}(1 +
\mu(\delta))$, the sum over independent subsets against the sum over all
subsets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Fernández--Procacci criterion; formalised]\label{thm:fp}
Let $\mu \ge 0$ on $\Lambda$ and suppose the Fernández--Procacci
condition holds on $\Lambda$. Then $Z(\Lambda) \neq 0$, and for every
$x \in \Lambda$,
$\lvert Z(\Lambda \setminus \{x\})\rvert \le (1 + \mu(x))\,
\lvert Z(\Lambda)\rvert$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (as formalised)]
We follow the inductive argument of Fialho \cite{Fia20}, in two stages,
both division-free strong inductions on the cardinality of
$S \subseteq \Lambda$. Write $Q_S = Z_S(-p)$ for the alternating gas at
activities $p = \lvert w \rvert$, and $\Xi_A = Z_A(\mu)$.

\emph{Stage 1.} $Q_S > 0$, together with the ratio comparison against
the $\mu$-complements:
$Q_{S \setminus x} \, \Xi_{\Lambda \setminus S} \le
Q_S \, \Xi_{\Lambda \setminus (S \setminus x)}$ for $x \in S$.
The single-deletion comparisons telescope along the incompatibility
neighbourhood of $x$ inside $S$; the deletion identity applied to
$\Xi$ at the point $x$ adjoined to $\Lambda \setminus S$, the
submultiplicativity $\Xi_{A \cup B} \le \Xi_A \Xi_B$ (independent sets
split injectively into their parts in $A$ and $B \setminus A$), and the
FP hypothesis $p_x \Xi_{N^*(x)} \le \mu_x$ close the induction. Taking
$S = \Lambda$ gives $Q_{\Lambda \setminus x} \le (1 + \mu(x))\,
Q_\Lambda$ and $Q_\Lambda > 0$.

\emph{Stage 2.} The comparison $Q_S \le \lvert Z_S(w) \rvert$, together
with the transferred ratio bound
$\lvert Z_{S \setminus x}(w)\rvert \, Q_S \le
Q_{S \setminus x} \, \lvert Z_S(w)\rvert$ — the finite-volume instance
of the minoration principle of Scott--Sokal \cite[Thm.~2.10]{SS05}. The
induction runs on the same recursion, with the triangle inequality in
place of positivity. Combining the two stages yields the theorem.
\end{proof}

Since the FP condition is the weakest of the three hypotheses,
Theorem~\ref{thm:fp} subsumes the nonvanishing statements of
Theorems~\ref{thm:dob} and~\ref{thm:kp}. The combinatorial layers of the
convergent Ursell-series expansion of $\log Z$ — Ursell functions, the
tree--graph bound, and the Mayer/cluster recursion — are formalised in
Sections~\ref{sec:ursell} and~\ref{sec:mayer}; the remaining analytic
layer is the identification of the iterated recursion with the
convergent series and the summability estimate over clusters.

\section{Ursell functions and the tree--graph bound}\label{sec:ursell}

The convergent expansion of $\log Z$ is a series over \emph{clusters}:
tuples $(\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_n)$ of polymers weighted by the Ursell
function
\[
  \varphi^{T}(\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_n)
  = \sum_{\substack{G \subseteq \mathcal{E}(\gamma) \\ G \text{ connected
  spanning}}} (-1)^{\lvert G \rvert},
\]
the alternating sum over connected spanning subgraphs of the
incompatibility graph $\mathcal{E}(\gamma)$ on the index set
$\{1, \dots, n\}$ (edges between indices of incompatible polymers, up to
the conventional $1/n!$). The combinatorial heart of every convergence
proof for this series is Penrose's tree--graph inequality \cite{Pen67},
which is now formalised (\texttt{KPLean/Ursell.lean}):

\begin{theorem}[tree--graph bound;
\texttt{abs\_ursellSum\_le\_treeCount},
\texttt{abs\_ursellInt\_le\_treeCount}]\label{thm:penrose}
For every finite set $H$ of non-diagonal edges on a finite nonempty
linearly ordered vertex set,
\[
  \Bigl\lvert \sum_{\substack{G \subseteq H \\ G \text{ connected
  spanning}}} (-1)^{\lvert G \rvert} \Bigr\rvert
  \;\le\; \#\{\text{spanning trees } T \subseteq H\}.
\]
In particular, $\lvert \varphi^{T}(\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_n) \rvert$ is
bounded by the number of spanning trees of the incompatibility graph.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof as formalised]
By a partition scheme in the sense of \cite[\S2.2]{SS05}. Fix the least
vertex as root. For a connected spanning $G \subseteq H$, the
breadth-first layers $\ell(v) = d_G(\mathrm{root}, v)$ assign to each
non-root vertex its \emph{Penrose parent}: the least neighbour in the
layer below. The edges $\{v, \mathrm{par}(v)\}$ form a spanning tree
$\tau(G)$ (\texttt{penroseTree}), and two layer-rigidity facts —
$\tau(G)$ has the same layers as $G$ (\texttt{lvl\_penroseTree}), and in
$\tau(G)$ each non-root vertex has its Penrose parent as unique lower
neighbour (\texttt{parentSet\_penroseTree}) — show that the fibre
$\tau^{-1}(T)$ is exactly the set interval $[T, R(T)]$, where $R(T)$
consists of those edges of $H$ joining equal layers, or joining a vertex
$v$ to a vertex $u$ of the layer below with $u \ge \mathrm{par}(v)$
(\texttt{penroseExt}; lemmas \texttt{penrose\_forward} and
\texttt{penrose\_backward}). On a nontrivial set interval $[A, B]$ the
alternating sum $\sum_{A \subseteq S \subseteq B} (-1)^{\lvert S \rvert}$
vanishes (\texttt{sum\_Icc\_neg\_one\_pow}), so at most one $\pm 1$
survives per fibre — and the fibres are indexed by spanning trees
(\texttt{penroseTree\_isTree}).
\end{proof}

The sanity values $\varphi^{T}(\gamma_1) = 1$ and
$\varphi^{T}(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) = -1$ for an incompatible pair are
proved as \texttt{ursellInt\_single} and \texttt{ursellInt\_pair}. The
graph theory used (walks, distances, connectivity, the characterisation
of trees by edge count) is \textsf{mathlib}'s; the BFS layer structure,
the Penrose parent map and the interval decomposition are ours.

\section{The Mayer expansion and the cluster recursion}\label{sec:mayer}

The bridge from the criteria of Section~\ref{sec:consequences} to the
cluster series of $\log Z$ is the exponential structure of $Z$, whose
finite, purely algebraic layer is now formalised
(\texttt{KPLean/Mayer.lean}) over an arbitrary commutative ring of
weights. First, the Mayer graph expansion \cite{MM41}:

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{Z\_eq\_sum\_graphs}]\label{thm:mayer}
\[
  Z(\Lambda) = \sum_{S \subseteq \Lambda}
  \Bigl(\prod_{\gamma \in S} w(\gamma)\Bigr)
  \sum_{G \subseteq \mathcal{E}(S)} (-1)^{\lvert G \rvert},
\]
where $\mathcal{E}(S)$ is the set of incompatibility edges inside $S$.
\end{theorem}

The inner alternating sum is $1$ or $0$ according to whether
$\mathcal{E}(S)$ is empty; it replaces the independence indicator.
Second, splitting each Mayer pair $(S, G)$ along the connected
component of a fixed polymer:

\begin{theorem}[cluster recursion; \texttt{Z\_cluster\_recursion}]
\label{thm:cluster}
For every polymer $\gamma_0$,
\[
  Z(\Lambda) = Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma_0\})
  + \sum_{\gamma_0 \in B \subseteq \Lambda}
    \Bigl(\prod_{\gamma \in B} w(\gamma)\Bigr)\, \varphi(B)\,
    Z(\Lambda \setminus B),
\]
where $\varphi(B) = \sum_{G} (-1)^{\lvert G \rvert}$ runs over the
connected spanning subgraphs of the incompatibility graph of $B$
(\texttt{ursellSetSum}; $\varphi(\{\gamma\}) = 1$ and
$\varphi(\{\gamma, \delta\}) = -1$ for an incompatible pair).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof as formalised]
Expand $Z(\Lambda)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:mayer} and split the outer sum
by $\gamma_0 \in S$; the $\gamma_0$-free part re-sums to
$Z(\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma_0\})$. The remaining Mayer pairs $(S, G)$
are fibred over the connected component $B$ of $\gamma_0$
(\texttt{component}), and each fibre is in explicit bijection with the
pairs of a connected spanning graph on $B$ and a Mayer pair on
$\Lambda \setminus B$: walk lemmas (\texttt{walk\_stays},
\texttt{walk\_transfer}) show that no edge of $G$ crosses the component
boundary (\texttt{fiber\_split}), that the restriction of $G$ to $B$
connects $B$ (\texttt{component\_conn}), and that the component of the
recombined pair is again $B$ (\texttt{component\_union}). Weights and
signs factorise along the split, and the inner double sum re-sums to
$Z(\Lambda \setminus B)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:mayer} in pair form
(\texttt{Z\_eq\_sum\_pairs}).
\end{proof}

The closed form of the iteration is also proved: the \emph{cluster
factorisation}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{Z\_eq\_sum\_clusterCollections}]\label{thm:fact}
\[
  Z(\Lambda) = \sum_{C} \prod_{B \in C}
  \Bigl(\prod_{\gamma \in B} w(\gamma)\Bigr)\, \varphi(B),
\]
the sum running over all collections $C$ of pairwise disjoint nonempty
subsets of $\Lambda$ (\texttt{IsClusterCollection}) — the finite
exponential formula in set form, by strong induction on
$\lvert \Lambda \rvert$ along Theorem~\ref{thm:cluster}: collections
without a $\gamma_0$-block live over $\Lambda \setminus \{\gamma_0\}$,
and collections with (by disjointness exactly one) $\gamma_0$-block
split bijectively into that block and a remainder collection over the
complement.
\end{theorem}

A companion \emph{bridge lemma} (\texttt{ursellInt\_eq\_ursellSetSum})
identifies the tuple-level Ursell function of an injective tuple with
the set-level sign sum $\varphi$ of its image, via the sign-preserving
relabelling bijection $G \mapsto \mathrm{Sym}^2(\gamma)(G)$ of
summation ranges. Together with the symmetrisation over labelled
tuples these two feed the exponential formula of
Section~\ref{sec:exp}; the series itself and its convergence in the
small-weight regime are the subject of Section~\ref{sec:series}.

\section{The cluster series and its convergence in the small-weight
regime}\label{sec:series}

The cluster series of $\log Z$ is now available as a genuine analytic
object (\texttt{KPLean/ClusterSeries.lean}): with
\[
  c_{n} \;=\; \frac{1}{(n+1)!}
  \sum_{(\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_{n+1}) \in \Lambda^{n+1}}
  \varphi^{T}(\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_{n+1})
  \prod_{i} w(\gamma_i)
\]
(\texttt{clusterOrderSum}, \texttt{clusterCoeff}), the series is the
infinite sum $\sum_{n \ge 0} c_n$ over orders, defined as a
\texttt{tsum} (\texttt{clusterSeries}); order one evaluates to
$\sum_{\gamma \in \Lambda} w(\gamma)$
(\texttt{clusterOrderSum\_zero}).

The input to convergence is a counting corollary of the Penrose scheme
of Section~\ref{sec:ursell}: a spanning tree is its own Penrose tree
(\texttt{penroseTree\_of\_isTree} — parent edges are tree edges, and
both sides have $\lvert V\rvert - 1$ of them), so spanning trees are
determined by their parent maps on $V \setminus \{\mathrm{root}\}$,
giving the \emph{root-tree bound}
\[
  \#\{\text{spanning trees in } H\} \le \lvert V \rvert^{\lvert V \rvert - 1},
  \qquad
  \lvert \varphi^{T}(\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_{n+1}) \rvert \le (n+1)^{n}
\]
(\texttt{treeCount\_le\_pow}, \texttt{abs\_ursellInt\_le\_pow}).

\begin{theorem}[convergence in the small-weight regime;
\texttt{abs\_clusterCoeff\_le}, \texttt{summable\_clusterCoeff},
\texttt{abs\_clusterSeries\_le}, \texttt{tsum\_pinned\_le}]
\label{thm:series}
Let $W = \sum_{\gamma \in \Lambda} \lvert w(\gamma) \rvert$ and suppose
$eW < 1$. Then $\lvert c_n \rvert \le (eW)^{n+1}$, the cluster series
converges absolutely with
$\lvert \sum_{n} c_n \rvert \le eW/(1 - eW)$, and the anchored absolute
series — the tuples with first coordinate a fixed polymer $\gamma_0$ —
is bounded by
\[
  \sum_{n \ge 0} \frac{1}{n!}
  \sum_{\substack{\gamma \in \Lambda^{n+1} \\ \gamma_1 = \gamma_0}}
  \lvert \varphi^{T}(\gamma) \rvert \prod_i \lvert w(\gamma_i) \rvert
  \;\le\; \frac{e \, \lvert w(\gamma_0) \rvert}{1 - eW},
\]
proportionally to the anchor weight and uniformly in the volume. The
$1/n!$ normalisation is the one relevant for volume differences of
$\log Z$: a tuple of order $n+1$ using $\gamma_0$ has $n+1$ possible
anchor positions, and $(n+1) \cdot 1/(n+1)! = 1/n!$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof as formalised]
Per order, the root-tree bound and the expansion of
$(\sum_\Lambda \lvert w \rvert)^{n+1}$ over tuples give
$\lvert \sum_{\gamma} \varphi^{T} \prod w \rvert \le (n+1)^n W^{n+1}$
(\texttt{abs\_clusterOrderSum\_le}); the factorial is absorbed by the
single term $i = n$ of the exponential series at $n+1$,
$(n+1)^n/(n+1)! \le e^{n+1}$ (\texttt{pow\_div\_factorial\_le}). The
term bound $(eW)^{n+1}$ follows, and with it summability and the
geometric tail bound by comparison. For the anchored series the same
argument runs with one coordinate pinned, splitting off the factor
$\lvert w(\gamma_0) \rvert$ via the \texttt{Fin.cons}/\texttt{Fin.tail}
reindexing of tuples (\texttt{pinnedOrderSum\_le},
\texttt{pinnedCoeff\_le}).
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:series} is the crude form of the summability estimate
over clusters: it requires smallness of the total weight rather than
the Kotecký--Preiss condition. Section~\ref{sec:exp} identifies what
the series computes; Section~\ref{sec:sharp} replaces the smallness
hypothesis by the Kotecký--Preiss condition itself.

\section{The exponential formula}\label{sec:exp}

The series of Section~\ref{sec:series} is in fact $\log Z$
(\texttt{KPLean/Exponential.lean}).

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{exp\_clusterSeries\_eq\_Z},
\texttt{log\_Z\_eq\_clusterSeries}, \texttt{Z\_pos\_of\_small}]
\label{thm:exp}
If $e \sum_{\gamma \in \Lambda} \lvert w(\gamma)\rvert < 1$, then
\[
  Z(\Lambda) = \exp\Bigl(\sum_{n \ge 0} c_n\Bigr),
  \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
  \log Z(\Lambda) = \sum_{n \ge 0} c_n,
\]
and in particular $Z(\Lambda) > 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof as formalised]
Fix a polymer $\gamma_\star \in \Lambda$ and, for a finite index set
$J$, pull the polymer system back along an assignment $h : J \to \iota$
(\texttt{PolymerSystem.pull}). Over the assignments valued in $\Lambda$
on a block $K$ and pinned to $\gamma_\star$ outside
(\texttt{pinnedTuples}) define the tuple sums $\mathrm{tupleZ}(K)$ and
$\mathrm{tupleU}(K)$, weighted by the independence indicator resp.\ the
set-level Ursell sum. Four identities link them to $Z$ and to the
series. First, the indicator is a partition sum,
$[\,S \text{ independent}\,] = \sum_{C \vdash S} \prod_{B \in C}
\varphi(B)$
(\texttt{indep\_indicator\_eq\_sum\_partitions}), proved by comparing
degree-$\lvert S\rvert$ coefficients in Theorem~\ref{thm:fact} over
$\mathbb{Z}[X]$ with the constant weight $X$ and lifting along
$\mathbb{Z} \to R$. Second, pinned assignment sums factor over disjoint
blocks (\texttt{combineOn}/\texttt{restrictOn} bijections), so
$\mathrm{tupleZ}(K) = \sum_{C \vdash K} \prod_{B \in C}
\mathrm{tupleU}(B)$ (\texttt{tupleZ\_eq\_sum\_partitions}). Third, along
the order isomorphism $\mathrm{Fin}\,\lvert B\rvert \simeq B$ the bridge
lemma gives $\mathrm{tupleU}(B) = c_{\lvert B\rvert - 1}
\cdot \lvert B\rvert!$ in the unnormalised form
(\texttt{tupleU\_eq\_clusterOrderSum}). Fourth, independence of the
pulled-back system on all of $\mathrm{Fin}\,m$ means injectivity
together with independence of the image, and the fibre over an
independent $m$-set consists of the embeddings
$\mathrm{Fin}\,m \hookrightarrow S$, hence has $m!$ elements; so
$\mathrm{tupleZ}(\mathrm{Fin}\,m)$ is $m!$ times the $m$-th layer of
$Z$ and $Z(\Lambda) = \sum_m \mathrm{tupleZ}(\mathrm{Fin}\,m)/m!$
(\texttt{tupleZ\_univ\_eq}, \texttt{Z\_eq\_sum\_tupleZ}).

On the analytic side, powers of an absolutely convergent series expand
as tuple sums; tuples with a vanishing component drop out, and the rest
regroups by total weight into compositions, giving
$\exp(\sum_j v_j) = \sum_m \sum_{k} (k!)^{-1}
\sum_{c \vDash_k m} \prod_i v_{c_i}$ (\texttt{exp\_tsum\_eq}), with
Fubini justified by the majorant $\exp(\sum_j \lvert v_j\rvert)$. The
bridge between the two sides is the multinomial count: ordered
partitions of an $m$-set with size profile $c$ number $m!/\prod_i c_i!$
(\texttt{sum\_orderedPartitionsF\_eq\_compositions}, by induction on the
number of blocks — split off the first block, group it by size, and use
$\binom{m}{d}(m-d)! = m!/d!$), and unordered ones a further $k!$ fewer,
since the fibre of $T \mapsto \operatorname{im} T$ consists of the
embeddings $\mathrm{Fin}\,k \hookrightarrow C$
(\texttt{sum\_orderedPartitionsF\_eq}); together
\texttt{sum\_partitionsOf\_card}. Applying it to
$\mathrm{tupleZ}(\mathrm{Fin}\,m)$ identifies the $m$-th coefficient of
$\exp$ of the series with $\mathrm{tupleZ}(\mathrm{Fin}\,m)/m!$; the
sum over $m$ terminates at $\lvert \Lambda\rvert$ because there are no
larger independent sets, and equals $Z(\Lambda)$.
\end{proof}

We note that \textsf{mathlib} does not supply the multinomial count:
\texttt{Multiset.bell} is \emph{defined} multinomially, and the
statement that it counts partitions is an explicit \texttt{TODO} in the
file that introduces it.

\section{The sharp Kotecký--Preiss estimate}\label{sec:sharp}

Theorem~\ref{thm:series} needs the total weight to be small. The sharp
estimate replaces that by the Kotecký--Preiss condition itself.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{tsum\_pinned\_le\_of\_kp}]\label{thm:sharp}
If $\sum_{\delta \nsim \gamma} \lvert w(\delta)\rvert e^{a(\delta)}
\le a(\gamma)$ for all $\gamma \in \Lambda$, then for every
$\gamma_0 \in \Lambda$
\[
  \sum_{n \ge 0} \frac{1}{n!}
  \sum_{\substack{\gamma \in \Lambda^{n+1} \\ \gamma_1 = \gamma_0}}
  \lvert \varphi^{T}(\gamma) \rvert \prod_i \lvert w(\gamma_i) \rvert
  \;\le\; \lvert w(\gamma_0) \rvert\, e^{a(\gamma_0)},
\]
uniformly in the volume.
\end{theorem}

The textbook route to this bound counts labelled trees with prescribed
degrees, which needs the Prüfer correspondence and Cayley's formula —
neither of which is in \textsf{mathlib}. The formalisation avoids both,
by carrying trees as \emph{parent maps} rather than edge sets: since
only an upper bound is wanted, an \emph{injection} from spanning trees
into parent maps suffices, and the Penrose scheme of
Section~\ref{sec:ursell} already provides one — the map $T \mapsto
\mathrm{par}_T$ is injective on spanning trees because
$\tau(T) = T$ (\texttt{penroseTree\_of\_isTree}), exactly the argument
behind the root-tree bound. This is \texttt{treeCount\_le\_card\_rootedTrees};
composed with Theorem~\ref{thm:penrose} it gives
\texttt{pinnedOrderSum\_le\_treeCoeff}, bounding the anchored Ursell sum
by $\lvert w(\gamma_0)\rvert$ times a weighted sum over rooted trees
whose edges all join incompatible polymers (\texttt{treeSum}).

\begin{proof}[Proof as formalised]
A rooted tree decomposes at its root: the subtrees hanging below the
children of the root partition the non-root vertices
(\texttt{subtreeOf\_image\_mem\_partitionsOf}), and each is itself a
rooted tree (\texttt{restrictParent\_mem\_rootedTrees}). Splitting off
one block at a time — the block containing a fixed vertex — turns the
tree sum into
\[
  \mathrm{treeSum}(A) \;\le\; \sum_{a_0 \in B_0 \subseteq A}
  \mathrm{blockFactor}(B_0)\, \mathrm{treeSum}(A \setminus B_0)
\]
(\texttt{treeSum\_le\_peel}). Its proof fibres the tree--assignment
pairs over the triple (block of $a_0$, its root, the value there); for a
fixed triple each pair splits into block data and outer data, injectively,
so the fibre embeds into a product of \emph{two} tree--assignment sets
and is bounded by a product of two tree sums (\texttt{fibre\_sum\_le}).
Peeling one block at a time avoids the dependent choice functions that
peeling all blocks at once would require. Induction on the vertex count,
together with the matching split of a partition sum
(\texttt{sum\_partitionsOf\_peel}), converts this into a bound by the
partition sum of block factors. Relabelling invariance
(\texttt{treeSum\_eq\_treeCoeff}) collapses each block factor to a
function of its size alone, and the multinomial count of
Section~\ref{sec:exp} — reused verbatim — sorts the partition sum into
compositions, giving the recursion
\[
  \frac{g_n(\gamma_0)}{n!} \;\le\; \sum_k \frac{1}{k!}
  \sum_{c \vDash_k n} \prod_j v_{c_j}(\gamma_0),
  \qquad
  v_m(\gamma) = \sum_{\delta \nsim \gamma} \lvert w(\delta)\rvert
  \frac{g_{m-1}(\delta)}{(m-1)!}
\]
(\texttt{treeCoeff\_div\_le}). Finally, induction on the truncation
height bounds every partial sum: the composition sum is at most
$\exp S$ with $S = \sum_m v_m(\gamma_0)$, the induction hypothesis
replaces the neighbour terms by $e^{a(\delta)}$, and the Kotecký--Preiss
condition contracts $S$ to $a(\gamma_0)$
(\texttt{treeTrunc\_le\_exp}).
\end{proof}

\section{A corrected claim}\label{sec:kp}

An earlier version of this note asserted that Theorem~\ref{thm:kp} was
\emph{not} obtainable from the product form, on the grounds that the
telescoping argument produces products of factors $1 + \mu(\delta)$ which a
sum hypothesis does not control, and supported this with a two-polymer
computation: for mutually incompatible polymers with equal weights and
equal $a$, the sum condition permits $w = a e^{-a}/2$, while the product
condition at $\mu = e^{a}-1$ demands $a/2 \le 1 - e^{-a}$, failing beyond
$a \approx 1.59$.

The computation is correct but refutes only the substitution
$\mu = e^{a}-1$. The substitution $\mu = \lvert w \rvert e^{a}$ used in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:kp} is the standard comparison (it appears in
\cite{FP07} and in \cite{SS05}), and it succeeds — on the same example,
the Dobrushin condition at the extremal Kotecký--Preiss weight reads
$(1 + a/2)^2 \le e^{a}$, which holds for all $a \ge 0$. The error was one
of quantifier order: from the failure of one substitution we inferred the
failure of all. What \emph{is} true, and what the example continues to
show, is the strictness of the hierarchy in Theorem~\ref{thm:hier}: a
single self-incompatible polymer of weight $\tfrac12$ satisfies the
Dobrushin condition with $\mu = 1$, while $\tfrac12 e^{a} \le a$ has no
solution, so the product form is genuinely more general than the sum form.
We record the episode because it is a specimen of what formalisation is
for: the incorrect general claim could not be formalised, and the attempt
to do so produced the correct proof instead.

\section{Related work}

We are not aware of prior formalisations of cluster expansion material in
any proof assistant. On the neighbouring shelves, \textsf{mathlib}
\cite{mathlib20} supplies the combinatorial and analytic prerequisites; the
recent formalisation of Brownian motion \cite{BM25} built Gaussian measure
and Kolmogorov extension infrastructure relevant to constructive field
theory; and the PhysLean effort \cite{HepLean} is assembling high energy
physics definitions in Lean. The exposition of Balaban's renormalisation
group method by Dimock \cite{Dim13a,Dim13b,Dim13c} is the natural blueprint
document for the larger programme, in the sense of the blueprint-driven
formalisations pioneered around \cite{LTE}.
